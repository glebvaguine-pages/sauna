✓ Sauna Sizing Recommendation: 6×7 ft is OPTIMAL

The 6×7×7 ft sauna is the professional industry standard for home use. This section explains why the 6 ft width (vs 5 ft) makes a critical difference in comfort, safety, and usability, and why it's worth the modest additional cost.

Why 6 ft Wide Changes Everything

❌ 5 ft Wide (NOT RECOMMENDED)

Interior Dimensions: 5 ft W × 7 ft L × 7 ft H

Volume: 245 ft³

⚠️ Problem Zone:

  • Upper Bench (24" deep): Takes up 2 ft of the 5 ft width
  • Lower Bench (18" deep): Takes up 1.5 ft
  • Remaining width in front: Only 2.5 ft
  • Heater + Guard Rail: Requires ~30" width minimum
  • Result: Heater footprint (2.5 ft wide) nearly fills the available floor space

❌ Tight Squeeze: Only ~1–1.5 ft free floor space on each side of heater. Users have minimal room to step around heater guard rail. Not ideal for comfort or safety.

✓ 6 ft Wide (RECOMMENDED STANDARD)

Interior Dimensions: 6 ft W × 7 ft L × 7 ft H

Volume: 294 ft³

✓ Comfortable Zone:

  • Upper Bench (24" deep): Takes up 2 ft of the 6 ft width
  • Lower Bench (18" deep): Takes up 1.5 ft
  • Remaining width in front: 2.5 ft
  • Heater + Guard Rail: ~30" width + clearance
  • Result: Comfortable 6–12" clearance on each side

✓ Proper Spacing: ~2 ft of usable floor space between bench and heater guard. Users can safely walk around, adjust position, and load rocks. Meets safety and comfort standards.

Key Considerations by Size

5×7 ft Sauna - Best For:

  • Solo use: Single person enjoying solitude (no space concerns)
  • Couples: Two people on benches, minimal floor movement
  • Budget-conscious: ~$700–$1,000 less expensive
  • Tight gazebo layout: Fits snugly if gazebo space is limited

⚠️ 5×7 ft Drawback: The front area becomes cramped with heater + guard rail. Users can't comfortably move around the heater, and the space feels claustrophobic for more than 2 people. Loading sauna rocks or exiting when the heater is on becomes trickier. **Only recommended for solo/couple use with realistic expectations.**

6×7 ft Sauna - Best For:

  • Family use: 2–3 people comfortably (one on lower bench, one on upper, standing room)
  • Social sauna sessions: Friends can sit and stand without feeling cramped
  • Long-term comfort: More circulation = better for heat distribution and user safety
  • Standard commercial sizing: Aligns with typical home sauna dimensions (gives you reference design standards)

✓ Recommendation: **6×7 ft is the better choice** for most users. The +$700–$1,000 investment buys significantly more usable space, better heater performance (larger unit handles volume better), and room for 2–3 people safely. The extra 1 ft of width transforms the layout from "tight" to "comfortable."

Phase 2: Insulation & Vapor Barrier

Duration: 2–3 working days
⚠️ MOST CRITICAL PHASE
⚠️ DO NOT SKIP OR RUSH: A continuous foil vapor barrier is what makes your sauna energy-efficient and prevents moisture damage. Poor barrier = 30–40% heat loss + interior rot in 3–5 years. Every seam MUST be taped with aluminum foil tape. This is non-negotiable.

Aluminum foil tape (not duct tape): Withstands 180–200°F with no degradation. Duct tape fails in sauna heat.

Overview

Install R-13 wall insulation and R-19 ceiling insulation, then cover all interior surfaces with FOIL-FACED vapor barrier (aluminum, never plastic), taping every seam and penetration. For a 7×5 ft space (~245 ft³), proper insulation is critical for heat retention.

Key Steps & Checklist

✓ Vapor Barrier Installation Tips:
  • Use a staple gun with 1/4" staples on 4" centers to hold barrier in place while taping
  • Aluminum foil tape ONLY (not duct tape)—it withstands sauna heat; duct tape fails in 6–12 months
  • Overlap seams by 2–3 inches; tape all overlaps on both sides for full sealing
  • Seal all penetrations (electrical boxes, vents, pipes) with foam sealant before taping
  • Test seals by running hand over every joint—you should feel no air movement

Phase 6: Exterior Cladding & Finishing

Duration: 5–7 working days
⚠️ Requires dry weather
⚠️ FURRING STRIPS ARE CRITICAL: The 3/4-inch air gap behind composite panels prevents moisture trap and rot. Without furring strips, water will eventually damage interior structure (cost: $10,000+ repair). Install 1×2 pressure-treated strips vertically on 16" centers over house wrap, then attach WPC boards to furring strips. This is non-negotiable.

Air Gap Function: Allows water that penetrates the outer boards to drain down and out via small weep holes at bottom trim. Interior structure stays dry.

Exterior Strategy

  1. House Wrap: Weather barrier, prevents water penetration
  2. Furring Strips: 1×2 lumber creates 3/4" ventilation gap
  3. Composite Cladding: Grey WPC boards, installed vertically
  4. Trim & Finishing: Corner pieces, door/window casing, top trim

Key Steps & Checklist

✓ Composite Cladding Advantages:
  • No finishing required—no stain, paint, or annual maintenance
  • Fade-resistant to UV and weathering (20+ year lifespan)
  • Clean aesthetic—grey WPC (wood-plastic composite) complements gazebo timber perfectly
  • Installed vertically (not horizontal) sheds water more effectively
  • Lightweight compared to wood, easier for DIY installation

Alternative: Reclaimed wood or cedar exterior (higher maintenance, natural appearance). Composite is recommended for low-touch long-term durability.
